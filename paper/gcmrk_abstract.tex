Gene co-expression analyses identify ``good'' modules by a correlation criterion,
yet standard pipelines detect modules with greedy algorithms that optimize other
quantities and only measure correlation afterwards. We present Genetic Clustering
by Metric (\tool{}), an open-source Python tool that closes this gap by treating
module detection as maximum-likelihood inference under an explicit generative
model and solving it globally. We show that the widely used absolute-correlation
criterion is, up to a constant and the sample-size factor, the profile
log-likelihood of a block-diagonal one-factor Gaussian in which each module is a
single regulator with equal-magnitude $\pm$ loadings, and that it is the
constrained member of a family whose general form allows $q$ factors with free
loadings. \tool{} maximizes the likelihood with a memetic genetic
algorithm---a population search hybridized with a greedy refinement that
reassigns genes after the fact, a move the agglomerative clustering at the core
of co-expression pipelines cannot make.

We evaluate on synthetic designs, on data generated by processes that violate the
model, and---most importantly---on the benchmark of Saelens et al., which pairs
real expression compendia with curated module definitions. Across
\SaelensNDatasets{} such datasets spanning three organisms, \tool{} attains the
best mean recovery of any method tested (F1 $\SaelensLoglikScore{}$, winning
\SaelensLoglikWins{} of \SaelensNDatasets{} outright), beating
Leiden and agglomerative clustering on every dataset ($p=\SaelensLeidenP{}$) while
remaining statistically indistinguishable from WGCNA ($\SaelensWgcnaBeaten{}$,
$p=\SaelensWgcnaP{}$). On replicated synthetic designs it leads every contemporary
detector at moderate noise, and it isolates unstructured genes far better than any
competitor (ARI $\NoiseGeneMemeticARI{}$ versus $\NoiseGeneLeidenARI{}$ for
Leiden).

Because the model is explicit we can also ask when it is wrong. Across
\MisspecTotal{} data-generating processes that violate different assumptions, the
constrained criterion fails outright where modules are not driven by a single
dominant signal, and we show this is misspecification rather than search failure,
because the optimizer finds partitions of \emph{higher} likelihood than the ground
truth. Freeing the loadings repairs it completely on those processes, leading all
\MisspecTotal{}. That advantage does not transfer: on all \SaelensNDatasets{} real
datasets the free-loading model is \emph{worse} than the constrained one
($p=\SaelensFactorP{}$) and far more expensive, so we recommend the constrained
criterion as the default and the factor model where module members are known to
respond at different strengths or to several regulators. \tool{} depends only on
NumPy and SciPy and exposes one swappable-metric interface with single- and
multi-objective modes.
